### C. Housing, safety, and daily life

Once the university city and campus are confirmed:

- Choose a first stay that is safe, well-connected to campus and essential services, and cancellable or short-term for the initial weeks.
- Compare the *total monthly cost*, not rent alone: utilities, internet, deposits, transport, groceries, laundry, insurance, and any building fees.
- Inspect the actual unit or use a trusted representative; verify the landlord/agent, tenancy terms, deposit handling, inventory, repair responsibility, and early-termination conditions.
- Avoid sending a substantial deposit before independently verifying the property and payment recipient. Be wary of pressure, unusually low rent, and requests to pay an unrelated account.
- Test commute routes at realistic class/work times. Consider late-night transport, pedestrian access, lighting, building access controls, and emergency routes.
- Identify nearby clinics/hospitals, pharmacies, grocery stores, public transport, and the university's emergency/security contacts.
- Confirm medical insurance coverage, exclusions, waiting periods, and whether it satisfies university/pass requirements.
- Keep passport and pass copies in secure digital storage; keep originals protected. Set up a local SIM, emergency contacts, and a backup payment method after arrival.
- Review official travel and local safety advice before departure; reassess neighbourhood suitability using current, specific information rather than broad country stereotypes.

### D. Finance and runway

Do not set a budget using guessed online averages. Build it from the actual campus city, household needs, housing quotes, university fees, and insurance requirements.

Create a monthly budget with these lines:
- Housing and utilities
- Food and household essentials
- Local transport
- Mobile and internet
- Healthcare and insurance
- University fees and study-related costs
- Visa/pass, document, and renewal costs
- Work equipment/software
- Flights, baggage, and initial setup
- Emergency/contingency allowance

Calculate three scenarios:
- **Lean:** essential costs only; no Malaysian income.
- **Base:** realistic costs and only confirmed recurring income.
- **Stress:** one income interruption plus an unexpected medical, travel, or housing cost.

**Runway formula:** liquid funds available for relocation and living expenses ÷ monthly essential shortfall = approximate months of runway. Keep relocation/setup money separate from the emergency reserve. Do not count uncertain job prospects, prospective consulting, credit limits, or unconfirmed income as cash.

Before booking:
- Decide the minimum reserve that must remain untouched.
- Agree on a monthly spending ceiling and who pays which costs.
- Confirm how income will be received and transferred, exchange fees, card access, and access to funds if one account/card fails.
- Ask a qualified tax adviser about Pakistani tax residency, Malaysian tax obligations, cross-border remote income, and any employer reporting requirements. Do not assume that overseas clients or overseas bank accounts make income tax-free.

## 4. Weekly operating rhythm

A sustainable weekly allocation during preparation:
- **Income protection:** remote-work agreement, delivery, and client/company communication.
- **Employment:** a few tailored applications and direct recruiter/employer follow-ups, rather than high-volume generic applications.
- **Consulting:** targeted conversations and one improvement to the offer/portfolio each week.
- **Relocation:** one concrete unresolved university, immigration, housing, or finance question closed each week.
- **Review:** update the budget, runway, application tracker, and decision log every weekend.

Avoid spending the entire preparation period researching possibilities. Every week should produce a concrete artifact or decision: a written university answer, a pass clarification, a budget, a CV revision, a qualified lead, or a verified housing option.

## 5. Decision gates and stop conditions

Do not proceed with a non-refundable move if any of these remain unresolved:
- The university cannot confirm the required physical arrival/enrolment timeline.
- The intended residence/entry route is unclear or unlikely to be approved in time.
- The household cannot cover the conservative initial period without relying on unauthorized or speculative income.
- Housing or travel payments cannot be independently verified.
- The remote-work arrangement is assumed but not agreed, or creates unresolved employer/security issues.

If the Malaysian job search produces no offer, the plan should still function: preserve approved remote income, reduce costs, maintain a legal job-search route, and expand personal remote consulting only where permitted. If remote income is interrupted, revisit the move budget and timing immediately rather than hoping local work will appear after arrival.
